The **Speed Test** page shows your last speed test in full: your speeds and responsiveness, whether your connection delivers your [Internet plan](/user-guide/settings/dashboard/#internet-plan), and the latency of your connection while it is idle and while it is busy. It replaces the Network Quality Report of earlier versions.

To open it:

- choose **Speed Test** under **Measurements** in the sidebar of the [PeakHour Advisor](/user-guide/advisor/);
- or click the **ISP** column on the [Internet Dashboard](/user-guide/main-view/internet-dashboard/#isp), then click **Open Full Report ›**.

The page works with the **AI Advisor** turned off. It needs macOS 26 or later. On macOS 14 and 15, the same results are in the [Network Quality Report](/user-guide/main-view/internet-dashboard/#network-quality-report) on the dashboard.

## Run a speed test

Click **Run Speed Test** at the top of the page. The button reads **Testing…** while the test runs. Before your first test, the page shows **No Speed Test Yet** with the same button.

PeakHour uses Apple's network quality test. It fills your connection with several connections at once to a nearby Apple server, so other traffic slows down for a few seconds. To run tests on a schedule, turn on [Measure Network Quality](/user-guide/settings/dashboard/#network-quality).

Every test goes into the [History](/user-guide/advisor/history/#speed-tests), also while the AI Advisor is off.

## The figures

The top of the page shows the time of the test and the interface it used, for example *Wi-Fi*, then four figures:

| Figure | What it shows |
|--------|---------------|
| **Download** | The highest download speed that the test reached, with several connections at once. |
| **Upload** | The highest upload speed that the test reached. |
| **Responsiveness** (↓ and ↑) | Round-trips Per Minute (RPM) while the test fills the connection, for download and for upload. Higher is better. A pill gives the band — see [How the grade works](/user-guide/advisor/grade/). |

With an [Internet plan](/user-guide/settings/dashboard/#internet-plan) set, each speed has a bar such as **82% of plan**. The bar is green from 85% of your plan, yellow from 70%, and red below that. Hover over the bar to see your plan speed.

Click the **i** beside a figure or a section heading to read what the measurement means.

## Your plan

With a plan set, a verdict under the speeds says where your speed stops. It reads your download speed:

| Verdict | When | What to try |
|---------|------|-------------|
| **Your connection delivers your plan speed.** | The last test reached 85% of your plan or more. | Nothing. A test seldom shows 100%, because the protocols and the test server take a part of each link. |
| **This Mac's Wi-Fi limits the speed.** | This Mac's Wi-Fi link carries less data than your plan. A Wi-Fi link carries about 60% of its Tx rate as data. | Use a cable, or move closer to the access point. |
| **This Mac's cable connection limits the speed.** | This Mac's Ethernet link carries less data than your plan. An Ethernet link carries about 94% of its speed as data. | Use a faster Ethernet port or adapter. |
| **This Mac's Wi-Fi may limit the speed.** (on a cable: **This Mac's connection may limit the speed.**) | The last test is below your plan. A speed test also measures this Mac's own link, so that link can be the limit. | Run a speed test over a cable at the router. |
| **Your connection is below your plan speed.** | Your last 3 tests in 7 days, over a link faster than your plan and without a VPN, have a middle result under 70% of your plan. | Run a speed test over a cable at the router. If the result stays low, contact your ISP with the results. |

A test through a VPN gives no verdict below your plan, because the VPN adds its own limit. Tests from before you set the plan don't count.

### Add your plan

If you haven't set a plan, the page asks once, after a speed test: **Compare with your plan** — *Enter the speeds that your internet plan promises.* It fills in the lowest common plan speed at or above each speed that the test measured. Change the speeds to match your plan, then click **Save**. **Not Now** hides the question for good.

You can set or change the plan at any time in [Settings → Dashboard → Internet Plan](/user-guide/settings/dashboard/#internet-plan). Without a plan, the page shows an **Add Your Internet Plan…** link that opens it.

## Idle Latency

How long a new connection takes to start while nothing else uses your connection. A bar shows the steps in order, and a row under it gives the time of each step and the total:

| Step | What it measures |
|------|------------------|
| **Base RTT** | The round trip to the test server while nothing else uses the connection. Every other step builds on it. |
| **TCP** | The time to open a connection to the server. It takes one round trip. |
| **TLS** | The time to set up encryption on the connection. Every secure website and app needs this step. |
| **H2** | The time to send one HTTP/2 request on the open connection and get the reply. |

Lower is better. This is the baseline for how quickly web pages and apps start to load.

## Latency Under Load

Latency measured while the test fills your connection, in one chart for download and one for upload. Spikes here indicate **bufferbloat** — congestion that delays interactive traffic like video calls and games.

Each chart reports three round-trip times:

| Value | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| **Min** | The lowest round-trip time observed during the test — your best-case latency under load, usually close to your idle latency. |
| **Avg** | The average round-trip time across the test — the most representative measure of how responsive the connection feels under load. |
| **Max** | The highest round-trip time observed — the worst-case latency, typically at peak congestion. A large gap between **Min** and **Max** is a sign of bufferbloat. |

If your latency under load is high, see [Turn on Smart Queue Management (SQM)](/troubleshooting/fix-a-grade/responsiveness-under-load/).

## Test

How the test ran:

| Row | What it shows |
|-----|---------------|
| **Interface** | The network interface the test used, for example Wi-Fi or Ethernet. |
| **Server** | The Apple server that ran the test. |
| **Protocol** | The protocol the test used. |
| **Flows** | The number of connections that ran at the same time in each direction. More connections fill a fast link. |
| **Data** | The amount of data the test transferred. |
| **Duration** | How long the test took. |

## Ask about the test

With [Private Cloud Compute](/user-guide/advisor/ask/) turned on, you can ask questions about the test at the bottom of the page. The answer knows the whole test, including the idle latency, the latency under load and your plan. The page suggests questions such as:

- *Do I get the speed of my plan?*
- *Do I have bufferbloat?*
- *What does responsiveness mean?*
- *Why does a new connection take this long?*
