PeakHour uses UPnP to automatically discover your router and monitor your internet bandwidth, without you having to type in an IP address or set up SNMP. If the Monitors list shows nothing under **Detected on This Network**, the most common reason is that UPnP is switched off at your router.

## What is UPnP, and is it safe to turn on?

UPnP (Universal Plug and Play) is a protocol that lets devices on your network discover and talk to each other automatically — see [UPnP](/troubleshooting/reference/upnp/) for the full explanation. PeakHour only uses it to *read* traffic counters from your router; it doesn't open any ports or change your router's configuration. Turning UPnP on for PeakHour is the same setting used by many game consoles, smart TVs, and other home apps, and is safe to leave on for normal home use.

## Where to find the setting

On almost every router, UPnP lives behind a single checkbox, usually under a heading like **Advanced**, **NAT**, or **Forwarding**. Log in to your router's admin page (typically by typing its address — often `192.168.1.1` or `192.168.0.1` — into a browser), then follow the steps below for your router.

### eero

1. Open the **eero** app.
2. Tap **eero Network** at the top, then **Network Settings**.
3. Tap **UPnP** and turn it **on**.

### Ubiquiti UniFi

1. Log in to the **UniFi Network** application (or UniFi OS console).
2. Go to **Settings → Internet**, or **Settings → Routing → NAT** on some versions.
3. Find **UPnP** and switch it **on**. On some UniFi versions this setting lives under the WAN interface's own settings instead.

### Netgear

1. Log in at `routerlogin.net` (or your router's IP address).
2. Go to **Advanced → Advanced Setup → UPnP**.
3. Check **Turn UPnP On**, then click **Apply**.

### ASUS

1. Log in to the router's web interface (often `router.asus.com`).
2. Go to **WAN → General** (**Advanced Settings → WAN** on older firmware).
3. Set **Enable UPnP** to **Yes**.

### BT (Smart Hub / Smart Hub 2)

1. Log in to the Smart Hub manager, usually at `192.168.1.254`.
2. Go to **Advanced Settings → Home Network**.
3. Under **UPnP**, tick **Enable UPnP**.

### Sky (Sky Broadband Hub)

1. Log in to the hub manager at `192.168.1.254` (the default password is on a sticker on the hub).
2. Go to **Advanced Settings → UPnP** (**NAT** on some hub versions).
3. Turn **UPnP** **on**.

### Xfinity (Comcast) gateway

1. Log in to the Xfinity admin tool at `10.0.0.1`.
2. Go to **Gateway → Connection → Advanced Settings** (or **At a Glance → Advanced**).
3. Find **UPnP** and set it to **Enabled**.

## After you turn it on

Give your router a minute, then reopen the Monitors list in PeakHour and check **Detected on This Network** again. If your router still doesn't show up, see [My router says it supports UPnP, but PeakHour can't see it or the data being reported doesn't seem right](/troubleshooting/upnp/router-not-detected-or-data-wrong/).

Can't find a UPnP setting at all? Some ISP-locked routers hide it or don't support it. In that case, you can monitor your Mac directly with [PeakHour Enabler](/user-guide/monitor-another-mac-peakhour-enabler/), or [enable SNMP on another device](/troubleshooting/faq/monitoring-compatibility/enable-snmp-on-mac-pc-linux/) on your network instead.
